## Gen Z's Rebellion: Networks, Nepotism and Negligence in Nepal 🔥🇳🇵

## 🔍 Project Overview

This project investigates how structural grievances, digital communication networks, and state repression interact to produce mass protest mobilisation, using Nepal's 2025 Gen Z uprising as a case study. The research asks: through what causal mechanisms did Nepal's political vulnerabilities and the 2025 Internet shutdown combine to produce an unprecedented regime-level outcome?

## 📊 Data Sources

- ACLED Conflict Event Data — 749 entries covering protest frequency, event types, and state violence in Nepal (2010–2026).
- V-Dem Dataset (v14)— Governance indicators including democratic indices, corruption, political violence, and media/internet control (2010–2025).
- #NepoBaby Social Media Data — 500 X posts (Sep–Oct 2025) capturing sentiment and network structure of the digital campaign.

---

## 🗺️ Methodology

- Temporal event analysis — Protest frequency and event-type distribution plotted across time to identify escalation phases.
- Spatial mapping — Monthly cumulative choropleth maps tracking geographical diffusion of contention across provinces.
- Digital network analysis — Sentiment classification and network centrality analysis of #NepoBaby posts.
- Process tracing* — Three causal mechanisms reconstructed: structural rot, digital spark, and state overreaction.

---
## 🔑 Key Findings

- 🦠Structural rot — Decades of elite cartel politics, chronic corruption, and acute generational exclusion created a large population receptive to mobilisation. 
- 🛜Digital spark  — The September 4, 2025 shutdown of 26 social media platforms occurred at peak #NepoBaby mobilisation, transforming diffuse online grievances into concentrated street action. 
- 🚨State overreaction — Lethal force against peaceful student protesters triggered a backfire effect, collapsing regime legitimacy within 48 hours. 
- ⚙️ Did it work? — The movement toppled the government and installed Nepal's first woman prime minister, but deeper structural transformation remains unresolved amid economic aftershocks.

---

## 🧩 Limitations

English-language source bias; weekly event aggregation may obscure micro-dynamics; limited official documentation due to event immediacy; digital trace data overrepresents urban, connected populations; single-case design limits generalisability.
